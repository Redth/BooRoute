Build BooRoute, a .NET MAUI app that helps neighbors organize
trick-or-treating on Halloween.

There are two experiences in the same app:

For families:
- Browse participating houses and view their details.
- See welcoming hours and status: Lights on, Out of treats, or Closed.
- Filter to houses welcoming visitors now.
- Filter by scare level: Friendly, Spooky, or Scary.
- Mark houses as visited, keeping that history private on the device.

For hosts:
- Register a participating house with a nickname, address, description,
  welcoming hours, and scare level.
- Optionally indicate quiet visits and step-free access.
- Edit the house and change its status.
- Make changes immediately visible in the family experience.

Use an in-app mock backend shared by both experiences, with async
service methods and 10 fictional houses. Persist changes and visited
state across restarts. No server, cloud setup, real authentication,
or cross-device synchronization. Clearly label the hosting flow
and dataset as a demo.

Make the demo work regardless of the time of day: seed some houses
with welcoming hours around the current local time. A house welcomes
visitors only when its status is Lights on and it is within its hours.

Make it visually polished and unmistakably Halloween:
midnight purple, pumpkin orange, cream text, house and pumpkin emoji,
generous spacing, and readable, accessible controls. Use text as well
as color for statuses.

Keep the first version focused: no embedded map, chat, notifications,
children’s accounts, or live location tracking.

Inspect the installed SDKs, MAUI workloads, and available simulators
before choosing a target. Follow existing repository conventions.
Use straightforward MVVM and dependency injection without
overengineering.

Give me a short plan, then implement it. Build and launch on an
available simulator, inspect the running app if automation is
available, and fix any issues. Be explicit about what you verified
and what remains untested.
